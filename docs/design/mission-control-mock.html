P5 — how the chief of staff draws you to a view

Both are implementable; they differ in who decides when you look. Fire each and watch the surface above. Neither is picked here — the trade-off is the deliverable.

alt A it takes the wheel

The chief of staff calls show(applet, target). The host switches the visible applet, scrolls the row into view, flashes it, and paints a banner saying who brought you here and why. The banner is dismissible and leaves no residue.

  • +Zero clicks. The thing that needs you is on screen the instant it happens.
  • +The reason travels with the jump, so the surface is never mysteriously different.
  • −It steals the surface. If you were three levels into Files, you lose your place.
  • −Two events in quick succession fight each other; the second wins and the first is never seen.
  • −Nothing is left behind to acknowledge, so a jump you missed while looking away is simply gone.

alt B it raises a flag

The chief of staff calls flag(applet, target, urgency). The host badges the tab and offers an inline show me in the conversation. The visible applet does not change. The badge survives until you look.

  • +Never steals focus. What you were doing survives what just happened.
  • +Badges accumulate, so three events in a minute are three counts, not one survivor.
  • +The badge is the acknowledgement receipt: it is still there when you come back from lunch.
  • −Costs a click, and a badge that is never clicked is a thing that never got seen.
  • −Needs an escalation rule, or a genuinely urgent event looks exactly like a routine one.

What is NOT settled: whether the two coexist under one policy (flag by default, take the wheel only when you asked for it or the surface is idle), and whether a third shape — peek, a transient overlay that reverts — is worth building. Carried into D3 of the design note.

what this prototype is claiming

  • P1The shell. The title is Mission Control. The topbar carries the title and the ⋯ menu, and nothing else — the struck-through cards | tiles marks where the toggle used to be pinned. Applet-owned controls live in the control rail at the right end of the tab strip, inside the applet host. The rail's contents change with the tab; the host paints the rail, the applet fills it.
  • P2Dashboard. The fleet view, unchanged in geometry — same fixed-height card contract, same three groups (wants you / working / done), same 3px state border. Its cards/tiles switch is now its own, in the rail. The #84 chips on cards are new: a proposal for the seam between applets. Click one.
  • P3Files. Changed files are bright and carry a status letter and a diffstat; unchanged files are dim and carry nothing. A closed directory containing changes wears one dot. changed only in the rail collapses it to just the work.
  • P4Pull Requests. Ten PRs across six statuses. Checks tick live (the rail says how stale the view is, and can be paused). Each row dismisses from the trailing edge; a dashed undo strip stands in its place, then it moves to dismissed at the bottom, dimmed, where it can be watched again. Dismiss means stop watching, never close the PR.
  • P5Attention. Two alternatives above, both live on this surface.
